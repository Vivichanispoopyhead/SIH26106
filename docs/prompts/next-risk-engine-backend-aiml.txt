SIH26106 - NEXT BACKEND/AIML FEATURE: DETERMINISTIC RISK ENGINE

The next backend/AIML feature should be the deterministic risk and verdict
engine, with the Gemini assessment treated as one signal among several.

Already available:

- Email parsing
- Indicator extraction
- SPF/DKIM/DMARC interpretation
- Received-chain analysis
- Gemini classification and confidence
- Evidence references
- Persisted analysis results

Missing:

- Risk score
- Severity level
- Verdict
- Contributing signals
- Final assessment confidence

Target flow:

Observed evidence
      +
Authentication signals
      +
Relay/header signals
      +
Extracted indicators
      +
AI assessment
      |
      v
Risk score + severity + verdict + contributing signals

Do not start enrichment, graph, maps, PDF, Redis caching, or WebSockets yet.
The risk engine makes the existing work feel like a complete product and can
be tested without additional external services.

Goal:
Add a deterministic risk engine that produces a threat score, risk level,
verdict, confidence, and contributing signals from the existing analysis data.

1. Add typed domain models.

Add a RiskAssessment model containing:

- score: integer from 0 to 100
- level: low, medium, high, or critical
- verdict: benign, suspicious, phishing, malware, fraud, or unknown
- confidence: nullable float from 0 to 1
- contributing_signals: typed list
- evidence_references: typed list

Add a RiskSignal model containing:

- code
- description
- points
- severity or category
- provenance
- evidence references

Do not merge risk score and confidence. They represent different concepts.

2. Define deterministic scoring rules.

Use a small, documented, configurable rule table. Do not use arbitrary
unexplained magic numbers scattered through handlers.

Initial signals may include:

- SPF fail: +20
- DKIM fail: +20
- DMARC fail: +20
- suspicious or malformed authentication state: +10
- extracted URL present: +10
- extracted IP indicator present: +5
- executable or double-extension attachment: +25
- multiple suspicious indicators: bounded additional points
- AI classification phishing: +30
- AI classification credential_harvesting: +30
- AI classification malware: +35
- AI classification fraud or payment_manipulation: +30
- AI classification benign: do not subtract enough to hide strong observed
  evidence; cap or neutralize the AI contribution explicitly
- AI confidence must scale the AI-derived contribution or determine whether it
  is accepted, but it must not directly become the risk score

Keep the exact weights modest and document them. The result must be
deterministic when the analyzer inputs are unchanged.

3. Define score levels.

Use a clear mapping such as:

- 0-24: low
- 25-49: medium
- 50-74: high
- 75-100: critical

Clamp the final score to 100.

4. Define verdict precedence.

The verdict must not be based only on the AI label.

Use deterministic precedence such as:

- malware or executable attachment with strong evidence -> malware
- phishing or credential-harvesting AI result plus URL/authentication signals
  -> phishing
- fraud or payment-manipulation AI result plus supporting signals -> fraud
- strong deterministic signals without a confident AI result -> suspicious
- no meaningful signals and a valid benign assessment -> benign
- otherwise -> unknown

Document the precedence and ensure an AI result is labeled AI-ASSESSED.

5. Define confidence separately.

Confidence should describe confidence in the final risk assessment, not
attacker identity.

A reasonable MVP rule:

- deterministic-only result: confidence based on the number and quality of
  deterministic signals
- AI-supported result: combine bounded deterministic confidence and AI
  confidence without allowing either to exceed 1
- no useful signals: confidence is null or a low explicit value according to
  the contract

Do not fabricate confidence when there is no basis for it.

6. Preserve provenance.

Every contributing signal must identify:

- whether it is observed, parsed, inferred, or AI-assessed
- supporting evidence references
- the source header/body/indicator when available

Do not create evidence references that do not exist.

7. Integrate the risk assessment.

Add the risk assessment to the existing AnalysisResult response:

{
  "risk": {
    "score": 0,
    "level": "low",
    "verdict": "unknown",
    "confidence": null,
    "contributing_signals": [],
    "evidence_references": []
  }
}

Keep the existing fields unchanged:

- analysis_id
- email_id
- case_id
- status
- ai_assessment
- authentication
- received_chain
- failure

Persist the risk result through both PostgreSQL and MemoryStore.

8. Handle partial AI failure safely.

If Gemini fails:

- retain authentication, relay, parser, and indicator results
- calculate a deterministic risk assessment from available signals
- set the overall analysis status to partial when appropriate
- do not invent an AI classification or confidence
- include the AI failure as a structured assessment failure

9. Add focused tests.

Test:

- no signals produces low/unknown with empty signal arrays
- SPF/DKIM/DMARC failures increase risk
- executable attachment increases risk
- URL indicators contribute to risk
- phishing AI result combines with deterministic signals
- benign AI result cannot erase strong observed evidence
- provider failure still produces a deterministic risk result
- score clamps at 100
- score levels are correct at all boundaries
- verdict precedence is deterministic
- confidence remains separate from score
- evidence references are preserved
- PostgreSQL JSON persistence shape and MemoryStore behavior
- existing upload, parsing, authentication, relay, Gemini, and HTTP tests remain
  valid
